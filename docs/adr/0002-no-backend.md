# No backend: on-device storage and client-rendered sharing

All data lives on the user's device and never leaves it. The Share Card is rendered on-device to a downloadable PNG rather than served as a hosted profile page, so there are no accounts, no database, no privacy policy and no moderation surface.

The decisive argument was distribution, not cost. People posting their Share Card is the only channel this app has, so putting a registration wall in front of the share action would sit directly across it — someone finishes a 30-day Chain, taps share, and gets an email field.

## Consequences

No multi-device. Deleting the app destroys the history irrecoverably, mitigated by shipping data export in v1 rather than deferring it as a power-user feature.

Sync is the intended paid tier if v1 holds — it is the only planned feature with a genuine recurring cost, and it never touches the free daily loop. Habit count is explicitly *not* a paywall: the honest ceiling is 2–3 Habits, so gating on quantity would sell a limit nobody wants to cross, and it would sell the exact overreach the underlying habit theory warns against.
